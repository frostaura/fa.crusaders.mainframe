# Feature Specification — Crusaders Mainframe

This is the canonical functional spec. Engineering implements this; deviations require a spec update first.

## 1. Roles & member groups
- **Player** — end-user, member of a tenant (club). Books, plays, pays, cancels.
- **Admin** — tenant-scoped operator. Can do anything a player can plus all admin operations within their tenant.
- **Platform admin** — cross-tenant operator (Dean by default). Can onboard new tenants and act as any tenant admin. *Implementation note: this is the same `User` entity with a `is_platform_admin` flag, scoped above `tenant_id`.*

**Member groups** — the one configurable concept for pricing and access (decision D2, 2026-09-23; it replaced the two-value member class of [client blueprint](./client-blueprints/Crusaders-Blueprint-2026-05-Rev2.md) A.7). A group carries booking windows, per-day caps and a booking horizon, **a package discount** (`package_discount_bps`, null = no say; P13) and **peak priority** (A.12) — a tri-state say: grants, withholds, or no say (the default). A member may be in several groups; the most restrictive rule wins — narrowest windows, lowest caps, **the lowest package discount among the groups that set one**, and **peak priority only if at least one of their groups grants it and none withholds it**; a group with no say on either the discount or the peak is ignored for it. A member in no group buys the club's packages at list price and has no peak priority. Groups no longer price anything else: since P13 a booking costs its playing minutes (§4).
- The **league group** (system key `league`, seeded as "League members") is what `LeagueOrSenior` became: peak priority, and (since P13) whatever package discount the club gives it in place of A.8's league rate. Renameable, not deletable, not archivable (either would re-price every league member at a click).
- **Admin reads, review round 1 (lane F3).** The group prices this lane added (`pricing`, `slice_price_*`) were removed by P13, because a booking no longer has a price other than its minutes (`minutes-and-packages.md` §3.8). `GET /admin/members/{userId}` carries `rejection_reason` for a rejected applicant and `review {reviewed_at, reviewed_by_user_id, reviewed_by_name}`. Each admin notice kind carries `others_receiving` — how many other active, confirmed admins are still told — so switching one off can warn that nobody will be. The night trace words the account's own story (applied, approved, turned down, suspended, reactivated, made or unmade admin, code reset, passkeys removed, password changed, temporary password) and no longer shows `credit.adjusted` beside the wallet row it duplicated.
- **A member's record includes the games they play as a partner (review round 2, C6).** `GET /admin/members/{userId}/bookings` and the detail's `next_bookings` list the bookings the member organises AND the ones they hold an accepted partner seat on; every row carries `role` (`organiser` · `partner`) and `organiser {user_id, name, is_self}`. `charged_minutes` stays the booking's charge (the organiser's), not the partner's share. An unanswered invite is not a game they are on. Since review round 6 (E6-10) every row also carries `sold_ends_at` (the end it was sold to — `ends_at` is cut by the no-show sweep) and `cost_minutes` (what THIS member is out on it: their own rows netted, so 13 on a swept 45-minute no-show at 30%, a partner's share on a partner row, 0 on a booking somebody else paid for). A no-show refunded in full because no box was reachable reads `status: "refunded_outage"`, never `noshow`, and is not in `counts_30d.no_shows` nor the club's no-show reports (E6-6; `Booking.OutageRefundedAt`, `Domain.NoShows`).
- **The change record carries every decision about a membership** (review round 1, B5/B10): approve, reject (with reason), suspend (with reason), reactivate, an admin temporary password (`member.password_reset`), the desk's code reset (`member.code_reset`, reason required) and admin rights granted or revoked (a reason is now required BOTH ways). Each row is staged in the decision's own save. A settings change's `changes[].field` is the `tenant_settings` key itself, the stable key the console maps to its own label. Viewing one member's activity or door history is recorded once per view: the same admin asking the same question of the same member within 30 s is folded into the first row (B19); exports are always recorded.
- Groups and memberships are admin-assigned and every change is written to the change record (`group.created` / `group.updated` with the changed fields / `group.members_changed`) **and**, for every member moved, to that member's audit trail (`member.groups_changed`, with the tariff before and after) — the successor of the class's `member.class_changed`.
- For one release the wire still carries a deprecated `member_class` (`LeagueOrSenior` for a member of the league group, else `Regular`) beside the new `groups`.

## 2. Registration & identity verification
1. Player visits the tenant's mobile web app and selects "Register".
2. Required fields, all mandatory:
   - Full name (2–120 characters)
   - Email address
   - Membership number (tenant-issued)
   - Password (at least 8 characters) — set here, not later; see §2.5
   - Photo upload (selfie, square, used as identification on the booking grid)
3. On submit:
   - Account created in `pending_approval` status.
   - Email verification link sent (24h expiry). The player can ask for it again from the sign-in screen — that request answers identically whether or not the address is registered, and spends any earlier unused link.
   - A membership number already registered in this tenant is refused as a named conflict (`membership_number_in_use`), exactly as a duplicate email is. Typing a digit of somebody else's club number is an ordinary mistake and must never surface as a server error.
   - Player sees "We've emailed you to confirm your address. After confirmation, an admin will review and approve your membership."
   - All tenant admins notified (email + in-app).
4. Admin reviews in their console. **Review is not gated on the applicant having confirmed their address** — an admin may reach a pending application first, and nothing about approval depends on the verification link having been clicked:
   - **Approve:** account becomes `active`. Welcome email goes out with a one-time magic-link sign-in (short-lived JWT in querystring, single-use, ≤15 min expiry). Consuming that link **confirms the address**, because clicking a link delivered to a mailbox is proof of control of that mailbox; an approved member is therefore never left able to sign in one way and refused the other. Webhook event fires.
   - **Reject:** admin must enter a reason (free text). Account becomes `rejected`. Rejection email sent with the reason. Webhook event fires.
   - **The transition is the status change; the email is a consequence of it.** Approve, reject, suspend and reactivate all commit the membership row and then attempt the notification, and a mail server that is down must not turn a committed transition into a failure the admin is shown — it used to, and an SMTP outage answered `POST /admin/members/{id}/approve` with HTTP 500 for a member whose row already said `active`. Each of the four therefore answers `member_notified`, and the console says which of the two things happened: approved and told, or approved and *not* told, so the club knows to pass the message on by hand. The same holds for the admin-issued temporary password, which is reached for precisely when mail is unreliable and must not claim an address it could not send to.
5. Once `active`, the player can **change** their password and/or enroll a passkey (WebAuthn) for biometric/touch sign-in convenience. (Earlier revisions of this section said "set a password" here, as though registration did not take one. It does — §2.2 — and the account-recovery paths in §3 are what set a password for an account that has none.)

## 3. Authentication
- **Primary:** email + password.
- **Convenience:** Passkey / WebAuthn (FaceID / TouchID / Windows Hello). Players may have multiple registered authenticators.
- **Email confirmation is a precondition of password sign-in**, and of nothing else. An unconfirmed address is refused with its own error code (`email_not_verified`), never collapsed into the not-approved refusal — they are different problems with different repairs, and the sign-in screen offers the repair for each. The welcome magic link is the one ingress that does not require prior confirmation, because consuming it *is* the confirmation (§2.4).
- **A decision the club has already taken outranks an unconfirmed address.** A membership that is `rejected` or `suspended` is refused as exactly that (`not_approved`, carrying the status), whether or not the address was ever confirmed: confirming an address cannot un-reject an application or lift a suspension, so asking for it there offers a repair for a problem the member does not have — and following it through lands on "an admin will review your application", which for a rejected applicant has already happened and was a refusal. A membership still `pending_approval` is the opposite case and keeps the `email_not_verified` refusal, because confirming is precisely what it still owes. The sign-in screen names all three separately — suspended, rejected, awaiting review — and offers the resend only for the third.
- **A session outlives the access token (review round 1, B1).** Every sign-in (password, passkey, magic link, the dev admin shortcut) and the member's own password change also set an httpOnly cookie `crusaders_rt` (`Path=/api/v1/auth`, `SameSite=Strict`, `Secure` outside Development, 30 days sliding — `Jwt:RefreshTokenLifetimeDays`) holding an opaque single-use refresh token, stored only as its SHA-256 in `refresh_tokens` with its family, club and the session epoch it was issued under. `POST /api/v1/auth/refresh` accepts a valid bearer as before or, with none, the cookie alone: `200` with the login body and a rotated cookie; `401 session_ended` with the cookie cleared when it is unknown in this club, expired, revoked, below the account's epoch or the membership is no longer active; a spent token presented again revokes its whole family (replay) — except within 10 s of being spent, which is a second tab, answered `401 refresh_superseded` without revoking or clearing. Every epoch raise (rotation, below) revokes the account's families; `POST /auth/logout` revokes the presented cookie's family and clears it, with or without a bearer. The cookie is read on those two routes only, and refresh changes nothing a cross-site forger can use: its token is in the response body.
- **Every access token names its refresh family (review round 2, C1).** A token handed out by a sign-in or a refresh carries the claim `rfid` — the family of the cookie it was minted beside. A bearer refresh whose family has been ended (sign-out in any tab, sign out everywhere, an epoch raise, a replay) is `401 session_ended`, with no `Set-Cookie`: the tab that did not sign out can no longer mint a token, find the jar empty and start a new family — which resurrected the session the member had just ended. Sign-out revokes the cookie's family AND the presented bearer's, and **every bearer of an ended family is refused on every route** within the same 15 s cache the epoch uses — evicted by the revoke itself, so at once on this instance. Stream tickets carry the minting session's `rfid`, and an open stream (wallet, control board, monitoring) re-checks the family with the epoch on every keep-alive and closes when it is ended. A family that is merely rotating is live (the check is "no link revoked and one unexpired", never "an unspent link exists"), and a token from before C1, naming no family, refreshes as before. The client half: a sign-out bumps a session generation and a refresh reply that lands after it is discarded (frontend, F4).
- **Suspension ends the session that is already open, not only the next one.** Access tokens are short-lived and renewed in the background for as long as a tab is open, and the renewal re-applies the membership test the sign-in path applies. A member suspended at the desk is therefore signed out at their next renewal — without it, the sign-in screen refused them while their open tab renewed itself indefinitely and kept reading their wallet, their ledger and the schedule.
- **Magic link:** the welcome email link, plus the password-reset / account-recovery flow, is the only other ingress.
- **Admin-issued temporary password:** admins can generate a temporary password for any user in their tenant. It **forces a password reset on first use**, and that is enforced at the API rather than by routing the browser: a session authenticated with a temporary password is refused on every endpoint except the one that replaces the password (`403 password_change_required`). The temporary password stops working at the moment it is replaced, which is also the only way the forced state is cleared.
  - Earlier revisions called it "single-use". It is not single-use in the literal sense — a member who abandons the change screen can sign in with it again — and it must not be, or a mistyped new password would lock the account out entirely. What "single use" is protecting is that the password an admin saw on their screen and emailed in plaintext never becomes the account's standing credential, and the API refusal above is what actually guarantees that.
- **Forgot password:** standard email-link reset. The reset token is **valid for one hour and works once**. This is deliberately longer than the ≤15-minute magic link: a member clicks an approval link while they are waiting for it, and reads a reset email whenever they next open their inbox. (Earlier revisions said "same expiry rules as magic links", which the implementation has never matched.)
- **Passkey reset:** a player can deregister an authenticator and enroll a new one any time, while signed in. Lost-device recovery uses the magic-link flow.
- **Changing your own password:** available to any signed-in member with a password, requiring the current one. A wrong current password is refused as a bad field (`400`), never as an expired session — a `401` on an authenticated route is the client's sign-out signal, and answering one here would sign a member out for a typo. Every successful change emails the member, which is how they would learn that somebody else made it.

## 4. Minutes (the wallet)
The normative contract is [`minutes-and-packages.md`](./minutes-and-packages.md) (P13, settled by Dean 2026-10-01); this section is its summary.
- **The unit is the whole minute.** A balance is the sum of an immutable ledger of whole minutes (`ledger_entries.AmountMinutes`, `integer`); nothing holds a fraction of one and no balance is stored. Rand appears only where money changed hands: package prices, purchase rows, `ZarPaid` and reports.
- **A booking costs exactly its playing minutes** (`SliceMinutes × SliceCount`). There are no court prices, group prices or conversion rates. Every remainder is rounded down in favour of whoever pays (contract §1.2): splits leave the remainder with the organiser, penalties are `floor(charged × penalty_pct ÷ 100)`.
- **Members buy packages, not amounts.** Admins define packages (minutes + rand price; archived, never deleted). A member's shelf is the club's packages at their **lowest group discount** plus their own groups' packages at those groups' prices; the screen shows the saving against the smallest package. There is **no spend cap**, no free amount and no admin exemption (contract §3.6, §4).
- **The purchase is locked at checkout.** `POST /api/v1/minutes/purchase` writes the package, minutes, list price, discount and amount onto the `pending_topups` row from the live shelf, and refuses with `package_price_changed` (409) when the price the screen quoted differs. The PayFast ITN credits the **locked** minutes and checks the paid amount against the locked amount; it never reads the live package. Abandoned checkouts close on the cancel return or after an hour, and a late signed COMPLETE reopens them. See [`payments-payfast.md`](./payments-payfast.md).
- Ledger entry types (names and stored numbers unchanged by P13):
  - `top_up` (positive minutes, a PayFast package purchase — with `ZarPaid`)
  - `booking_charge` (negative minutes, source: **lights-on activation** per blueprint A.9, NOT booking creation — see §6)
  - `cancellation_refund` (positive minutes, source: mid-session cancellation after lights-on — rare; the common cancellation path produces no ledger entry because no charge happened yet)
  - `no_show_penalty` (negative minutes, source: missed grace window without lights-on)
  - `late_cancellation_penalty` (negative minutes, source: cancellation inside the grace window before lights-on)
  - `lights_purchase` (negative minutes, court time bought at the box as a walk-up)
  - `admin_top_up` (positive minutes, a package sold at the desk — with the rand received) and `admin_adjustment` (signed minutes, reason required)
  - `payment_reversal` (signed minutes, a gateway dispute/chargeback)
  - `share_transfer` (a member-to-member pair for a split booking, netting to zero — §5a)
- Members see their running ledger in minutes ("2 h 30 min", never decimals or "credits"); admins see every ledger in their club.
- **Held minutes (D11).** Minutes an organiser receives as partners' shares of a split booking are part of the balance but not spendable until that booking's share deadline, because a partner's withdrawal, a lowered or re-split share, a removal, a desk settle-outside or a late cancellation can hand them back. Every member payment checks *spendable* minutes (balance less held); the wallet, its live stream and the profile show `held_minutes` and when the earliest hold releases. Only an admin's explicit overdraft looks past it. See `players-and-splits.md` §3a.
- **The desk** sells a package (`POST /api/v1/admin/minutes/{userId}/sell`: a cash, EFT or offline-card code; the desk states the price it was shown, `quotedPriceZar`, and a price that moved since is 409 `package_price_changed`; the rand received defaults to that quoted price) and adjusts minutes (`/adjust`: goodwill, correction, refund outside the system or other; signed, reason required, may go below zero) as two separate routes, so a sale's package and money are never mixed with a signed goodwill figure (contract §3.7).

## 5. Court scheduling
- Each tenant has N courts (configurable, default 4 for Crusaders).

> **Crusaders client constraints** (see [blueprint](./client-blueprints/Crusaders-Blueprint-2026-05-Rev2.md)):
> - Slice length default **15 minutes** (B.4).
> - A slice costs its own minutes (P13; it replaced A.8's credit price) — A.9 still decides when it is charged.
> - Per-member-per-day cap: max 60 minutes total (A.4 + B.3). Same-day-empty-slot relaxation: a player at the cap can still book a slot starting today if it's still empty.
> - Peak-hours exclusivity: 16:00–19:00 reserved for members with peak priority (the league group, since D2) until 1 hour before slot start; inside that window any member can book if free (A.12). The refusal code is still `peak_hours_league_only`, and it names the member's groups that withhold priority (`withheld_by_groups`).
> - Admin always bypasses caps and peak-hour rules (A.6, A.11).

- The schedule is a grid: courts on the X axis, time slices on the Y axis, displayed for one day at a time.
- Slice length is configurable per tenant: 15m / 30m / 1h.
- Slice price is configurable per tenant per court (default flat across courts).
- Bookings open `T` days into the future (configurable, default 14).
- Operating hours per day per tenant: configurable (e.g. 06:00–22:00). Held as `tenant_settings.operating_hours`, a per-weekday map; the flat `day_start_minute` / `day_end_minute` pair is the default for any weekday the map does not name. **Enforced by `BookingService`, not only drawn by the grid** — a member who reaches the API directly is refused `out_of_hours` with that day's opening and closing times, because a slot sold outside hours is a slot the grid cannot draw and the member therefore cannot see, cancel or be refunded from the app. A walk-up bought while the club is OPEN is truncated at closing rather than refused; one attempted while the club is SHUT is refused `club_closed`, carrying that day's opening and closing times so the box can put them on the glass. It used to collapse into the catch-all `walk_up_refused`, which is in no firmware table, so a member at a locked club after hours read SEE THE DESK when there is no desk (finding M-F5). `club_closed` is deliberately a different code from the app's `out_of_hours`: that one means the slot you picked is outside hours and can be answered by picking another, this one means the club is shut right now and cannot. Admins bypass, so maintenance and events can be booked out of hours.
- Date picker lets the player navigate ±N days. Forward is bounded by `advance_booking_days`; backward the player may look at the same number of days into the past, read-only — a past day's slots are never bookable, and until there is a bookings list elsewhere in the product this is the only way a player can review a booking they have already played.
- **Slot states:**
  - `available` — green, clickable.
  - `reserved_by_me` — distinct color, shows my own name.
  - `reserved_by_other` — neutral color, shows the other player's photo + first name only.
  - `admin_block` — distinct color, label e.g. "Tournament", "Maintenance". Not bookable.
  - `peak_league_only` — inside the peak window (A.12), for a member the rule would refuse: rendered and inert, in its own colour, labelled for the rule rather than for the price. It becomes `available` once the slot is within `peak_advance_hours_for_regular` of starting, and it never appears for an admin or a member with peak priority (a `LeagueOrSenior` member before D2). Not a state the API stores — `GET /schedule` carries the window, the advance allowance and one flag saying whether *this viewer* is bound by the rule, and the grid derives the state. *(Added 2026-09-07. The rule was enforced only in `BookingService`, so a finder counted 44 cells a day drawn to a Regular member as `available` — "green, clickable", same cursor, same "Book Court 2 at 17:00 for 50.00 cr" label — each of which opened a modal quoting court, time, cost and balance-after before refusing after Confirm. `available` is a promise the grid makes; the client cannot infer this one, because the member's class is on no member-facing payload and `peak_hours` is admin-only.)*
  - `out_of_hours` — greyed out and not bookable. *(This line used to read "greyed out, not displayed", which asks for two contradictory things. Rendered-and-inert is the reading that keeps the grid legible: a row that vanishes leaves the player unable to tell a closed club from a bug, and a booking that already exists outside hours — an admin block, a walk-up that ran to closing, a slot sold before the rule was enforced — must still be visible to its owner. The grid draws the day's operating window plus whatever range any existing booking on that day occupies.)*
- **Booking action:**
  - Tap an `available` slot → confirmation modal showing court, time, duration, cost, current balance, balance after.
  - The modal has an explicit confirm control and **no timer**: it stays open until the player confirms or dismisses it. A confirmation that disarms itself after a few seconds is a price the player was not given time to read.
  - On confirm: balance must cover cost. If not, prompt to top up.
  - Every refusal carries a stable snake_case `error` code and the figures its sentence needs — `past_time`, `too_far_ahead`, `slot_taken`, `invalid_duration`, `insufficient_balance`, `peak_hours_league_only`, `per_day_cap_exceeded`, `out_of_hours`, `no_time_available`, `duplicate_request`, `court_not_found`. **A rule refusing a booking is never a 5xx.** The client maps codes to sentences; a code or an exception is never shown to a player.
  - **A busy server says busy (R18).** A request that could not get a database connection is `503 service_busy` (`door-gate.md`, the error contract): the screen says the club's system is very busy right now and to try again in a moment — never that it is down, restarting or didn't answer, and Try again stays. A booking sheet still reads the day back after it (a 5xx is never a "no"), and its sentence is count-aware: over several slots it says "these" and "the slots", never one slot's sentence.
  - Booking is committed atomically: ledger debit + booking row + confirmation email + receipt PDF + webhook event.
- **Multi-slice bookings:** a player can select consecutive slots on the same court before confirming (e.g. two 30m slices = 1 hour). Each slice is its own booking row for clean cancellation accounting.
- **Recurring bookings:** out of scope for v1.

### 5a. Players and splits (D11–D13)
Up to four players per booking (seats 1–2 team A, 3–4 team B; seat 1 the organiser), members, guests or an open invite-link seat. **The organiser pays the whole price at booking and every settlement rule is unchanged**; each partner's share (equal, ratio or fixed amount — partners rounded down to whole minutes, the organiser takes the remainder) moves to the organiser as a `ShareTransfer` ledger pair when they accept, and follows the organiser's `Owed` thereafter — refunded on a cancellation (the late-cancel penalty is the organiser's alone), shared pro rata on a no-show. Unpaid shares are payable until the booking's share deadline — `share_lapse_hours` (48) after the session, frozen when the booking first gets players — then lapse to the organiser's cover — no debts, no booking blocks, and a lapsed share is never collected afterwards (only an admin's explicit collect can). A partner accepts the figure they were shown (a share raised since is refused as `share_changed`); an open link seat is claimed only through its link; shares the organiser receives are held until the deadline so a return never overdraws them (§4). An accepted partner counts against their own daily cap, may open the court and the door, and may withdraw only outside the late-cancel window. Only a live seat shows a member the booking; a member the organiser removed cannot rejoin by link, and one who declined or was removed is not re-invited by the organiser (an admin may). Invites per booking are capped by `player_invites_per_booking` (8). Finding other members by name is opt-in (`findable`). Normative detail and the endpoint catalogue: `players-and-splits.md`.

### 5b. Play (D14 MVP)
What a member did on court, kept for them alone. **Match results** (squash PAR-11, win by two after 10–all; best of 3 or 5, chosen by whoever records) may be recorded by a player of a booking the court box checked in — a door scan is not attendance, and a cancelled game takes no result and counts none (R18) — within `result_record_days` (7) of its end, singles or doubles with guests allowed; one member of the other side confirms or disputes, or it confirms itself after `result_autoconfirm_hours` (48); a dispute waits for an admin to remove or keep it (`/admin/results`, in the change record). One live result per booking. **Personal stats** (sessions, time on court from attendance, match and game record, who you play with most, recent form), a **weekly streak** in the club's week (Monday–Sunday local, DST-safe) with earned rest weeks and a yearly injury-pause allowance, twelve **badges** for attendance and participation — never for spending — a self-set **monthly court-time ring**, and one-shot **celebrations** in the PWA. All private to the member: opponents see only the result on the shared booking; nothing is public, nothing nags, and only "a result to confirm" is emailed. Normative detail, the endpoint catalogue and the lane partition: `play.md`.

## 6. Court activation, lights-on, and the moment of charge

> **Note on charge timing.** **The debit happens at booking, not at lights-on.** `BookingService.CreateAsync` reserves the slot and posts the `BookingCharge` debit in the same unit of work, so a lost slot race cannot leave a member paying for a court they did not get. Cancelling before check-in refunds in full, less the late-cancellation penalty inside the penalty window; once someone has checked in there is nothing back. This **contradicts blueprint A.9**, which debits on lights-on; the deviation is the founder's decision, is recorded in `client-blueprint-deltas.md`, and needs committee sign-off before go-live. The normative statement of what a booking owes in every terminal state is `settlement-model.md` §2 — read that, not this note, when the answer has to be right.
>
> **At-court payer override** (blueprint B.2): after the booked player is identified at the touchpad, a *different* identified player can press "I'll pay" and that player's account is debited instead. The booking row records both `booked_by_user_id` and `paid_by_user_id`. Both are audit-logged and emailed.

## 6a. The biometric peripheral & no-show detection
- Each court has a physical biometric peripheral attached to the on-site machine. The .NET app talks to it via `IBiometricDevice` (see `biometric-abstraction.md`).
- The court lights are switched by the **court box** (ESP8266 bridge + Mega relay bank with an independent auto-off timer), which decides locally from an online activation or its cached heartbeat roster and pulls everything from the API; it is never a webhook destination. `lights-controller.md` (webhook-driven Arduino) is superseded — see `device-serial-protocol.md` and *the court box and its key* below.
- When a player has a booking that becomes "live" (current time falls within the booked slice):
  1. The on-court UI awaits a biometric match.
  2. On a successful match against the booked player, the mainframe fires a `booking.lights_on` webhook to the Arduino, the relay flips, and a `LightsOn` event is recorded.
  3. If no successful match within `grace_window_minutes` (configurable, default 5) past the slice start:
     - The booking is marked `no_show`.
     - `no_show_penalty` ledger entry written: `penalty_pct * slice_cost` (default 30%).
     - No-show email sent to player.
     - Webhook event fires.
  4. If attendance evidence arrives *after* the no-show was finalized — an `activation` event a box queued through an outage — the sweep is **reversed**: the booking returns to the attended state and `Owed(b)` returns to the full price, so the member is neither refunded nor penalised for a court they played. `settlement-model.md` §2, S6. (This is the C2/C6 pair: the earlier behaviour left a member who had played both refunded *and* penalised.)
- **Stand-in / different player at the court:** if biometric-matches a player who does NOT have the booking, the system rejects the lights-on. UI on the courtside terminal shows "No active booking for this user." Admin can override at the tenant console.
- **Only an `active` membership opens a court, and that holds on every device path.** A membership that is `suspended`, `rejected` or still `pending_approval` may not light a booked court and may not buy a walk-up — the suspension email and the console's own suspend dialog both promise it in those words. Enforced in four places because there are four doors: the app's booking route (`member_suspended`, 403), the activation candidate set (`/device/activate`, the courtside PIN/QR/biometric match — all of which answer their ordinary `no_booking`), the walk-up (`/device/walkup`, answered `not_recognised` **before** anything is charged, so the box arms no button window and shows SEE THE DESK), and the cached heartbeat roster, so the rule survives an outage. **`POST /me/qr/generate` refuses one too** (`member_suspended` / `member_not_active`, 403, the booking route's own codes): the code it would mint is inert at every one of those four doors, but the screen showing it tells the member it will "start a walk-up on a free court, or pay for someone else's time", and a promise nothing keeps is a defect whether or not money moves. The screen is the other half of that fix — it replaces the code, the QR and the walk-up switch with the reason, because a raw refusal code on a member's phone would be worse than the promise it removes. The box never names the reason: whoever is standing at the court is not necessarily the member. **The one deliberate exception is `/device/events`** — an activation a box already decided from a roster fetched before the club's decision is still recorded as attendance, because the relay has run and the member has played; refusing it would refund a court that was used and waive the penalty, which is the opposite of what the suspension is for.
- **Lights stay on for the duration of the booked slice(s).** End-of-slice signals the lights to turn off; if the booking is back-to-back, lights stay on through the boundary.
- **Peripheral failure mode.** A box is *unreachable* when the API has not heard from it **at all** for longer than `offline_after_seconds` — the same threshold the Devices page reads, derived from the club's own `heartbeat_seconds`. *Heard from* is any authenticated call the box makes: `/heartbeat`, `/events`, `/activate` or `/walkup`. It used to be the heartbeat alone, and a box that was actively switching courts on through `/activate` therefore counted as silent — the club got the high-priority alert while the box was mid-scan, the Devices page read "last seen 11m ago" beside a court it had just lit, and the waiver below then refunded that court's no-shows and waived the penalty (finding L-F5). Anything that breaks the periodic POST while leaving request/response working — a stalled firmware timer, a rate limit, a proxy that buffers — converted into free courts and alert noise, and no screen said why. `PeripheralHealthWorker` sweeps for it, and three things follow:
  1. **The club's admins get a high-priority alert** — a real `X-Priority: 1` mail naming the box and the courts it drives, plus a `peripheral.unhealthy` webhook. Once per outage, and a `peripheral.recovered` when it comes back.
  2. **No penalty is charged for a booking the outage covered.** A booking whose court had no reachable box *at the moment its grace window closed* is refunded in full and charged nothing: `Owed(b) = 0`, `settlement-model.md` S5a. That is this clause's "auto-credited (no penalty)". **"No reachable box" is the same test as everywhere else in this section** — silent for longer than `offline_after_seconds`, measured at the grace deadline against the last heartbeat that preceded it — applied to **every** enabled box that drives the court, not to whichever one happens to be dark. A court with two boxes on it is dark only when both are past the threshold. A court the club has put no box on at all is not dark — there is no evidence either way — but a court whose boxes have **all been switched out of service** is, because `DeviceIdentity.ResolveAsync` refuses a disabled board `403` on all five `/api/v1/device/*` routes — `/heartbeat`, `/activate`, `/walkup`, `/events` and `/gate` — so the box is refused every route it calls, is no longer told which bookings it may open, and can never report anyone who played; that half was missing and charged those members the full penalty (finding M-F3, `settlement-model.md` S5a). **It is the box that is shut, not the court**: `/api/v1/court/{n}/pin/match`, `/qr/match` and `/biometric/match` are the shared courtside terminal, resolve a `Courts.CourtNumber`, never look at a `Device` row, and still set `CheckedInAt` — so three of the six ways in survive the switch, and the claim this bullet used to make — that no check-in of any kind could reach the club once the last box was switched off — was false for two rounds. The waiver is argued from what remains true rather than from that claim; `settlement-model.md` S5a, "The door is not shut everywhere" and "The waiver still stands", carries the argument and states its cost. **That half is bounded, and it took three tries to bound it in the right place** — `Enabled` carries no history, so the first version read "dark because it is switched off" as "dark for as far back as this board has existed", and one click waived every unsettled deadline back to the sweep's lookback floor on courts that had been working. The second bounded it by the board's own silence, treating `LastSeenAt` on a switched-off row as the switch-off instant; it is not that instant, it is the last heartbeat before it, and the court therefore read Lit for up to `offline_after_seconds` after the click while the board itself was already refused — a member whose court the club had shut a minute earlier paid the full `penalty_pct` and was mailed the plain no-show notice. **The switch-off instant is now recorded in its own column** (`devices.OutOfServiceSince`, stamped by `DeviceRegistryService.UpdateAsync` on the transition and cleared when the board goes back into service), and the rule reads: a deadline after that instant is out of service at once, because the club has declared the door shut and there is nothing left to wait for; a deadline at or before it is judged by exactly the silence test an enabled board gets, so the click re-prices nothing behind it. A board that never called in at all was never in service and shuts the door from the moment it existed. Pinned by `A_court_is_out_of_service_from_the_instant_the_switch_was_thrown`, whose two rows vary only where the switch fell relative to the deadline and both sit inside the window where the silence test alone answers "reachable", and by `A_switch_off_darkens_only_the_deadlines_the_board_had_already_gone_quiet_for` for the deadlines behind the switch. (A club that replaces a box and leaves the old row enabled has one box silent forever on that court, and the looser reading would waive every no-show there permanently — `settlement-model.md` S5a, "How many boxes have to be dark".) It is deliberately not "an outage row happens to span the deadline", and equally not "whatever the box's last heartbeat says right now": the first made the price depend on whether the health sweep had run yet, the second on whether the box had come back yet, and both priced identical facts two ways. A silence past the threshold is recorded when it *ends*, by the heartbeat or upload that ends it, so the evidence survives the recovery that would otherwise erase it. **And a box only speaks for deadlines it existed at** — see the bullet below, because auto-registration otherwise turned any spare board into a veto on this clause.
  3. **The affected players are told why** — a distinct email that explains the controller was offline, states the full refund and says no penalty was taken. Not the no-show notice: a member standing at a dark court has not failed to turn up. **There are two of these mails, because only one of the two dark states can be undone.** The outage notice tells the member the box uploads what it queued when it reconnects and the booking then settles as attended (S6); a board an admin has taken out of service is refused 403 on `/device/events` as well, so nothing it holds will ever arrive, the settlement is final, and repeating the reconnect promise would send a member who did play away to wait for a correction that is not coming. The out-of-service notice says so and points them at the desk. Pinned by `The_out_of_service_waiver_mail_does_not_promise_a_box_that_will_never_reconnect` and `The_outage_waiver_mail_still_promises_the_upload_for_a_box_that_can_come_back`.
- **A board that did not exist at the deadline is not evidence about it.** `AutoRegisterAsync` is the whole of provisioning: an unknown MAC that announces itself is put straight onto the club's first four courts, `Enabled` true, with nobody having confirmed that it drives any of them — that is what makes a box useful the moment it is switched on. Combined with "every enabled box on the court has to be dark", it silently reversed this clause: **one healthy unconfigured board vetoed the waiver on courts 1–4**, so a spare on the desk, or the replacement an admin plugs in while the dead one is still in the cupboard, made every member on a dark court pay the 30% for the club's own hardware. Under the older "any dark box waives" rule an extra board could only ever *add* waivers; the all-boxes rule gave an extra board the power to *remove* them, and neither spec said so. A box therefore counts, in **either** direction, only from the earliest instant it is known to have existed — the earliest of its row's `CreatedAt`, its `LastSeenAt`, and the start of any silence recorded for it. Before that it is skipped entirely rather than treated as dark, so a court whose only box arrived after the deadline is "not dark" for the same reason a court with no box is. Pinned by `A_board_that_appeared_after_the_deadline_does_not_block_the_waiver` and `A_board_already_installed_at_the_deadline_still_blocks_the_waiver`, which differ only in the board's install date.
  - **Not "auto-registered boxes never vote", which was the other candidate.** Excluding them from the veto alone would waive every no-show on a court whose only box is a healthy unconfirmed one — a club that has not been through its Devices page yet would collect no penalties at all. Excluding them in both directions, the way a disabled box is excluded, would deny §6a's protection to the members of exactly that club, which is the direction this clause exists to prevent. Existence is the narrower rule and it removes the actual exposure: every real instance of it — the bench spare, the replacement, the board an installer plugs in mid-week — is a board that appeared *after* the deadline it was voting on. What it leaves is a board that has sat auto-registered and heart-beating on guessed courts since before the booking; that one still votes, and the Devices page flags it as unconfirmed.
- **What §6a does *not* mean, and why.** It does **not** mean every booking in the outage window is cancelled and refunded on sight. The box is built to survive losing its uplink — it lights courts from its cached roster and queues the activations (see 4 above) — so "unreachable from the API" is not "the member was locked out". Refunding a court that was played would break `settlement-model.md` I2 and pay out for electricity the club burned. The refund therefore rides the no-show sweep, which is the only point at which the system concludes that nobody played; and if the box was in fact lighting courts, its queued activations arrive **on reconnect provided the box stayed powered**, reverse the sweep, and `Owed(b)` returns to the full price with no penalty either way (S6).
- **The case where that proviso fails, and what it costs.** The event queue is RAM (`src/iot/shared/lib/eventq/include/eventq/EventQueue.h`), so a box that lit courts from its cached roster and then lost power never uploads those activations. S6 never happens and the full refund stands on a court that was played — the single accepted exception to I2, recorded there and on the go-live checklist. It used to cost the member an unearned penalty, which they could see and dispute; it now costs the club and is invisible, which is why the outage alert does not tell an admin to power-cycle the box and the recovery mail does not promise that everything uploads.
- **Why the grace deadline is the instant that decides it.** A box that reconnected before the deadline uploads what it queued through the outage **provided it stayed powered** — the ring is RAM — so if the member played we usually already know it and the booking does not reach the sweep at all. The word is *usually*, not *always*, and the bullet above is the exception: the same power cut that ended the outage discards the queue, and that case is the one accepted I2 exception rather than something this clause rules out. A box still dark at the deadline is one whose evidence had no path home.

## 7. Cancellations
- A player can cancel any of their own bookings from the app.
- The cancellation modal clearly displays:
  - "Cancelling now is **free** — full refund of your minutes." (outside penalty window)
  - **OR** "Cancelling within the penalty window — **30% ({X} min) penalty** will be deducted, {Y} min refunded." (inside)
  - **OR** "You have already checked in — the lights have been on, so **nothing is refunded**." (after check-in; `settlement-model.md` §2 S4)
- **The figures come from the server, at the moment the modal opens** — `GET /api/v1/bookings/{id}/cancellation-quote`, computed through the same settlement the cancellation itself uses. They are not carried on the schedule payload and not re-derived in the client: the answer turns on the clock (the penalty window) and on attendance (a scan at the court), and both move after a schedule is loaded. The quote is a quote, not a reservation — the cancellation re-derives everything, and the confirmation states what actually moved.
- The confirmation after a cancellation names the actual figures returned by the cancel call. "Refund pending" is not an acceptable message: it was shown identically when 50 minutes came back, when 35 did, and when nothing did.
- The penalty window is defined as `≤ penalty_window_minutes` (configurable, default 15) before the slice start.
- On confirm:
  - Booking status → `cancelled`.
  - Ledger entries written (refund + optional penalty).
  - Cancellation email sent (with refund amount and any penalty explanation).
  - Slice returns to `available`.
  - Webhook event fires.
- **A cancel refused for good ends the sheet (R18).** `booking_not_open` (already cancelled), `not_cancellable` (played, a no-show or a hold), `booking_not_found` and `already_checked_in` drop the quote's figures, the waiver and the Cancel button, and the way out is a primary "Done" — pressing Cancel again could never work. A member's cancel of their own booking that is no longer open is told so by code, as the desk is, rather than "not found" (`minutes-and-packages.md`). A rate limit, a lost answer or a busy server keep everything, because they can be tried again.
- An admin can cancel any booking; refunds always go to the booking's player. Admin can choose to waive the penalty (logged with reason).

## 8. Admin operations
A tenant admin can:
- Configure tenant settings: court count, court names, slice length, penalty %, grace window, penalty window, advance-booking days, operating hours per weekday.
- Approve / reject / suspend members.
- Generate a temporary password for any member.
- **Run a biometric enrollment session for any member**, with the member physically present at a tenant machine that has a peripheral attached. Multiple finger / face templates per member supported. Re-enrollment replaces the previous template with full audit trail. See `biometric-abstraction.md` § Admin-led enrollment flow.
- **Book on behalf of any member.** Charges that member's minutes and never the admin's — a
  desk account's balance is zero permanently, because it never plays. If the member cannot
  cover the court the booking is **refused first**, with their balance and the price on the
  refusal; the admin can then override that refusal and take the member below zero. The
  override is deliberate, not a bypass admins get for being admins:
  - It is asked for **by name** — `allowNegativeBalance` on `POST /api/v1/admin/bookings` —
    on a second attempt, after the desk has read the two figures. The confirmation modal
    shows the member's name, their balance, the price and the balance it will leave, and the
    override button states that balance in its own label.
  - It is honoured **only when the actor differs from the payer**, so an admin cannot
    overdraw their own wallet with it and a member has no route to it at all: the field does
    not exist on the member's own booking request, and the service would refuse it anyway.
  - It is **logged** as `booking.created_on_behalf_overdrawn`, carrying the actor, the
    member, and the balance before and after. An override the member turned out to be able
    to afford is audited as an ordinary `booking.created_on_behalf` — the trail records the
    outcome, not the request.
  - Nothing else about the booking changes: one `BookingCharge` at the quoted price, so
    `settlement-model.md` S1 still says `Owed(b) = P` and I1 still bounds it. What goes
    negative is the **wallet**, which is a sum over the ledger and has never been constrained
    to be non-negative; an ordinary top-up nets against it, because there is no separate debt
    record to reconcile.
  - The member's confirmation email states the negative balance and asks them to top up.
    Being in the red is not a suspension and does not block anything by itself.
- Cancel any booking on behalf of any member (penalty waiveable with reason).
- Block a slot for an event (creates an `admin_block` entry; can be a single slot or a range). `POST /api/v1/admin/blocks` creates one, `DELETE /api/v1/admin/blocks/{id}` lifts it. A block reserves the court through the same overlap exclusion constraint a booking does, takes no money and writes no ledger row. It **cannot be laid over an existing booking** — a player who paid must be refunded deliberately, through a cancellation, rather than overwritten. Lifting a block cancels rather than deletes it, so the trail survives. *Recurring weekly blocks are out of scope for v1, alongside recurring bookings (§5); a range is created as one block per slot run.*
- The three admin actions above — book on behalf, cancel any booking with an optional waived penalty, and block — are reachable **from the schedule grid**, on the slot they act on. The endpoints existing without a screen is the same defect as the screen existing without the endpoints.
- Sell a package or adjust minutes at the desk (§4; contract §3.7 is normative and supersedes the next sentences where they differ). Historically: a top-up code (`top_up_cash`, `top_up_eft`, `top_up_card_offline`) is the club's revenue and must say how much money changed hands: `zarReceived` (≥ 0, whole cents) is required on those codes (`400 zar_received_required`), stored as the row's `ZarPaid` and counted in the revenue report's `net_zar` (review round 1, B7); other codes ignore it. `amount_out_of_range` names its `max`. Every ledger row (admin ledger and the member's wallet) carries `actor_name`: the person who moved the money on the owner's behalf — the desk's adjustment or top-up, a booking or cancel made for them, or (since R16 E16-7) the other member whose act moved a share — and null exactly for their own movements and the system's (B9). The row's `actorName` agrees with it whenever it is set, is the owner's own name for their own act, and reads "Automatic" (was "(unknown)") for the club's machinery. A `ShareTransfer` row also carries `counterparty_name` and `counterparty_user_id` — the other member of the pair — and reads member to member ("Sam → Alice") on the member's ledger and the admin ledger alike, never via the club treasury (review round 2, C5); the name is the membership's, never an address.
- View / export all transactions, bookings, members, no-shows.
- Configure the tenant's outbound webhooks.
- View peripheral health (uptime, last-seen, error rate per court).
- Configure the court-box **button map** — the club default and, per box, an override (below). Both live on the Devices page, not this one; see §8a.

### 8a. Court-box button map
A court box has eight physical buttons (`ButtonMap.ButtonCount`), wired A/B/C/D then 15/30/45/60. The Mega reports raw presses and the bridge decides, so any button can be mapped to any action: select a court (by position in the box's own court list, relays A–D) or grant a number of minutes (bounded by the relay hard cap, `ButtonMap.MaxGrantMinutes`).

- **Club default:** `tenant_settings.buttons`, edited on the **Devices page**, beside the per-box override it is the fallback for. (This line used to say the Settings page, and the build has never done that. The two maps are validated against each other on every edit — an override is refused when it reaches past its box's court list, and the club default is refused when any box running it would — so an admin editing one needs the other in front of them. Splitting them across two screens would mean reading a refusal on one page about a value set on another.) The default map is the wiring's original meaning, so a club that never touches it gets exactly what the hand-built box did.
- **Per-device override:** `button_map` on `PATCH /admin/devices/{id}`, stored as `Device.ButtonMapJson`. It is three-valued: absent leaves the override alone, `null` clears it (the box runs the club default), a list of eight entries sets it.
- **Validated per box, not per club.** The *effective* map — the override if any, else the club default (`ButtonMap.EffectiveFor`) — is checked against that box's court list on every edit: a map whose court button reaches past the box's list is refused with `button_map_selects_court_beyond_device_courts` and the map named. A one-court annex box and a four-court main box can therefore both be valid under one club default, each with its own override where needed; the old single tenant-wide validation had no valid configuration for a mixed fleet.
- **A box that registers itself is given a map it can honour.** Auto-registration stores the club's map trimmed to the box's court count when the club's map reaches past it, rather than leaving buttons that select nothing — the same rule as shrinking a box's court list by hand.
- **The heartbeat also sends `walk_up_max_minutes`** — `min(tenant_settings.walk_up_max_minutes, relay hard cap)`, the same bound `/device/walkup` enforces, so the box is never told a number the API will refuse. It was not sent, and the two screens that set these numbers did not know about each other: a club that lowered its walk-up maximum to 20 left every box still advertising 15/30/45/60 and the API refusing three of the four presses with `minutes_out_of_range` — the club's own configuration change, blamed on the member, at a court with nobody at the desk (finding M-F1). The Devices page now warns beside any minute button that exceeds the cap, naming the number and where it is set; it does not refuse the save, because the cap can be raised afterwards and a button map that anticipates that is not wrong.
- **The heartbeat sends the effective map** (`button_map` in the heartbeat response, `ButtonMap.EffectiveFor(device.ButtonMapJson, settings.Buttons)`), so the box never has to know whether it is running an override. The Devices screen renders the override editor only when the API carries the field.

### 8b. The court box and its key
One club, one key, and a box that introduces itself.

- **Authentication is one static key per club.** Every device request carries `Authorization: Bearer <device_api_key>`; the key resolves to a tenant and to nothing finer. An admin reads it from `GET /api/v1/admin/devices/api-key` (the Devices page) and pastes it into the box's `secrets.h`; `POST /api/v1/admin/devices/api-key/regenerate` replaces it, invalidating the old one on the very next request, so every box must be reflashed. Because the key cannot tell boards apart, **the device rate limit is charged per board by the body's MAC** (R16, `Security/DeviceRateKey`; 120 a minute per `device:{tenant}:{mac}`, else per club, else per address): one board spinning on a refusal exhausts only its own bucket, never every box and door in the club. Nor does it spend the club address's anonymous global bucket (R17): device requests have their own per-board and device-only per-address global buckets.
- **Identity is the MAC in the request body.** `mac` (twelve lowercase hex, no separators) names the board on `heartbeat`, `activate`, `walkup` and `events`. It authenticates nothing — the key does that — it only says which row a request is about.
- **Registration is a heartbeat.** An unknown MAC creates a `Device` for the key's tenant: `device_code` `box-<last six hex of the mac>`, the club's court numbers ascending up to the four the relay bank drives, the club's button map trimmed if it does not fit, audited as `device.auto_registered` and flagged `auto_registered: true` on the wire until an admin edits the row. The Devices page badges it so the guessed court numbers get checked.
- **Admin can edit, take out of service, and delete.** Deleting is a reset rather than a revocation: the board still holds the club key, so it re-registers with default courts on its next heartbeat — which is why it is useless for a box that is broken but still powered. **Out of service** (`enabled`) is the lever for that one: the API refuses a disabled box on every route the box itself calls, `PeripheralHealthWorker` stops alerting on it, and `DeviceOutageIndex` stops counting it when deciding whether a court is dark. The field had been on the API, on the typed client and in the settlement model since the row existed and **no screen ever sent it** (finding M-F3), so a box that was broken or unplugged for good alerted the club indefinitely about a silence nobody could stop, and — because a court is dark only while every enabled box driving it is dark — kept its courts dark for ever, waiving the 30% no-show penalty on them permanently. Note the other direction, which is money and which both the code and this sentence used to get wrong: taking the LAST box off a court leaves nobody able to check in **at the box** — `DeviceIdentity.ResolveAsync` answers 403 on all five `/api/v1/device/*` routes, though not on the courtside terminal's own `/api/v1/court/{n}/…` routes, which never look at a device row — while `CourtWasDark` read the court as "no enabled box, therefore no evidence, therefore not dark" and settled its no-shows at the full 30%. A court whose boxes have **all** been switched out of service is now dark (`settlement-model.md` S5a), so those bookings are refunded in full with no penalty; a court the club has put no box on at all is still not dark, which is the case that clause was written for. **The switch is not retroactive**: it darkens the court from the instant the switch was thrown, which is recorded on the row as `devices.OutOfServiceSince` — PascalCase, like `LastSeenAt` and every other column on that table; this line named it in snake_case for a round and no column of that name has ever existed in the code, the migration or the model snapshot — and not from the day the board was installed, or one click would re-price every deadline still inside the sweep's lookback window (S5a, "the switch itself does not reach backwards"). **Nor is it delayed**: from that instant the court is dark at once, because the club has declared the door shut and the API refuses the board on all five of its own routes, so there is no longer anything to wait `offline_after_seconds` for — reading the board's last heartbeat as the switch-off instead charged those members the full penalty for the first three minutes after the click. The console says most of this, per court and only where the court is left with no box in service: the out-of-service row names those courts and states the waiver, and the confirm on **Take out of service** warns before the click when it is the last box on a court. **Its wording has not caught up with the correction above** — `src/frontend/src/lib/deviceCoverage.ts` still tells an admin "nobody can check in there", which is the same withdrawn claim, and the money half of both sentences ("refunded in full with no no-show penalty") is correct. This note goes when that copy does.
- **The Devices page is a live view, not a snapshot.** It re-reads the boxes every 15 seconds — one beat of the fastest cadence the firmware will run — and offers a Refresh control and the time of its last read. It loaded once on mount and never again, which mattered because the peripheral-health alert email links to this page and asks an admin to watch the box come back: an open tab read "online" for a box six and a half minutes dark, long after the club had been emailed about it, while a second tab on the same URL read "last seen 6m ago" (finding M-F2). A box that has gone quiet on its own is shown as *not reporting*; a box an admin switched off is shown as *out of service* and never as offline, because nobody expects it to call in.
- **Gone:** discovery, claim codes, per-device keys, key rotation, reclaim, revoke, manual registration, request signing, the clock window and over-the-air updates. All of it existed to deliver or repair a credential an admin could never read back, and each link could strand a board in a cupboard. See `deployment.md`.

### 8c. Door gates
A second kind of device on the same surface: one ESP32 per door, a QR scanner, one relay on the lock. **`docs/specs/door-gate.md` is the behaviour oracle**; only what a reader of this file needs in order not to be misled is here.

- **It is the court box's shape with the money taken out.** Same club key, same `/api/v1/device` group, same auto-registration, same roster grammar — and no `court_numbers`, no button map, no walk-up and no activation, because a door has none of them. A board declares itself with an optional `kind` on its first heartbeat; absent means `court_box`, so every box in the field is untouched, and a board can never re-kind a row that already exists.
- **Walking through a door is not attendance.** A gate never stamps `CheckedInAt`, never reverses a no-show and never posts a ledger row — the member may be collecting a racquet or watching somebody else's match. This is the one place where the four-doors rule above does *not* extend to a new surface: a gate is a fifth door into the *building*, not into a court, and none of the money consequences in §6a follow it. A gate is likewise invisible to the settlement model: `DeviceOutageIndex` decides whether a court is dark from the boxes that drive it, and a gate drives none.
- **Only an `active` membership opens a door**, on the same not-Active-rather-than-Suspended rule as everything in §6a, and the refusal is named (`member_not_active`) rather than folded into `not_recognised` because the audit row is the only record a door leaves. Under the default `gate_access_policy` an active member needs no booking; under `booking_window` they need one inside `gate_early_entry_minutes` of its start — **except a club admin, who always passes that check**, because a club must not lock its own staff out of the building by changing a setting, and staff do not hold bookings for the hours they are working. There is no "operator" role in this product and never was: the exemption is `IsAdmin` and nothing else.
- **A booking's QR is not a building key.** A booking-activation code opens a door only while `now` is inside its own booking's window — `gate_early_entry_minutes` before the slot under `booking_window`, and under the default policy the same horizon in which the member's phone would show them that code at all. Outside it the door answers `too_early` before and `not_recognised` after, the latter deliberately indistinguishable from a dead code. Without that bound the code a member's phone showed them at 17:00 for a 19:00 court opened every door in the club at 03:00 the same night. A sixty-second ident code is unaffected: it names no booking. **The bound is on the WINDOW and not on the booking's status**, which it was for two rounds and which locked out the wrong people: the no-show sweep writes `NoShow` five minutes after a slot starts and `LightsCueWorker` writes `Completed` within fifteen seconds of its end, so the member running late and the member who had just played were both refused at the door while their codes were still live. `Booked`, `NoShow` and `Completed` all count, out to the instant that booking's own code dies; only `Cancelled` and `AdminBlock` — the two the CLUB decides — count for nothing. `door-gate.md` §5 carries the argument.
- **Offline, a gate admits rostered bookings and nobody else** — a sixty-second ident code cannot be in a cache fetched minutes ago, so an active member with no court booked gets in online and does not get in offline. That is the stated degraded mode, not a gap: the alternative is the club's whole membership roll on a board in a doorway. **A board an admin has not yet named gets no roster at all**, so its offline fallback starts when the door is named: auto-registration means anything holding the club key can invent a MAC and register a "gate", and answering that first heartbeat with the club's live access codes was handing the whole set to whoever asked.
- **A silent door gets a door's alert, never the court box's.** `PeripheralHealthWorker` sweeps both kinds, and the court-box alert tells an admin about courts, refunds and waived no-show penalties — every clause of which is false about a gate, which drives no courts and settles nothing. A silent gate says what is true instead: the door still opens for booked members off the list it holds, and for nobody once that list goes stale six hours later.
- **Egress is mechanical and is not this system's to provide.** Getting out of the building must not depend on the board, the relay, the API or the power. Whether the door is released when the power is out is a property of the lock, not of any software here. `door-gate.md` §11 and the go-live checklist carry both.

### 8d. The single rotating member code (D1) and pay-for at the box (D10)
**`docs/specs/member-code.md` is the behaviour oracle** and `src/contracts/device-protocol/membercode/` its corpus; this is what a reader of this file needs.

- **One code per member**, 19 characters, rotating every 30 s, computed on the phone from a secret the server hands out once per generation (`POST /api/v1/me/code/enrol`; `GET /api/v1/me/code` never carries it). The server keeps only each member's REF and generation (`member_codes`); the secret is derived from `MemberCodes:MasterKey`, which Production refuses to start without.
- **Every device path takes it** — `/activate`, `/walkup`, `/gate`, `/pay-for` — alongside legacy codes, checked on both halves at the instant the board read it (`scan_age_ms`). A code acts once per class (door, court, money; `member_code_uses`); a second acting use is `code_used`.
- **Rotation** (`generation += 1`) on a password change or reset, an admin temporary password, a suspension, `POST /api/v1/admin/members/{membershipId}/code/reset`, and **sign out everywhere** (`POST /api/v1/me/sessions/revoke-all`). **Every rotation also ends every session the member had open** by raising their session epoch (`users.SessionEpoch`, carried in every token) — before the change is saved and again after it, so a sign-in with the old password in between does not survive: an older token answers 401 on every route — within the 15 s the check is cached for, at once on the backend's own container — refresh and enrolment read the row (enrolment under a row lock), and open streams close. The desk's reset also removes the member's passkeys unless `revoke_passkeys: false` (sign-out-everywhere keeps them unless `revoke_passkeys: true`); both answers say whether they did. The member's own password change keeps the session it was made from: the answer carries a replacement `access_token` the client must adopt. Logout does not rotate.
- **Rollout is automatic.** `member_code_phase` moves `legacy → v1` the first time every enabled board reports `codes:m1` on its latest heartbeat, and `v1 → v1_only` `member_code_cutoff_days` (14–365, default 14) later. From `v1` bookings stop minting per-booking codes and rosters name players; from `v1_only` legacy codes are refused everywhere. Only **acknowledged** enabled boards vote (a court box an admin acknowledged, a door with a `gate_key`); an auto-registered board neither blocks nor tips it. An admin can move the phase by hand in the settings (the rollback lever): **a backward move sets a hold** (`member_code_manual_hold`, with who and when) that stops the heartbeat moving the phase forward until an admin moves forward again or clears it; back to `v1` restamps the cut-over and clears the cut-off. `GET /api/v1/admin/devices/member-codes` lists the boards blocking cut-over, the unacknowledged ones apart, and the hold; every device carries `capabilities`. The cut-off, automatic or by hand, expires every legacy access code (a rollback out of `v1_only` gives back the ones still in date), and from `v1_only` the courtside QR kiosk and the biometric payer override refuse legacy codes as every device path does.
- **Offline evidence is re-checked.** An uploaded `activation`/`gate_open` carrying a v1 code the server cannot reproduce — or one used twice by two different boards — is receipted but is not attendance, and raises the non-optional `code.forgery_suspected` notice. The same board's upload of a code it already acted on (its fallback of an online grant, or a retried upload) is a plain receipt — but an offline event clearly earlier than that board's online use is a second act and alarms, and the boards themselves refuse, without asking the server, a code they already acted on. A code of the previous generation is honoured for six hours after a rotation, never for a member not active at the scan; an event whose clock the server had to clamp is never attendance — recorded `unverifiable`, except that a reuse by another board or a failed ON still raises the alarm. The booking is the one whose sold window holds the scan, back-to-back slots included. A board reporting without `codes:m1` after cut-over raises `device.codes_capability_lost`.
- **Pay-for at the box (D10)**: scan, press somebody's booked court, press again. The payer takes over the booking's charge — a refund to the booker and a debit to the payer, posted together through the payer-override path — and both are emailed; a booking nobody has started is lit for its remainder. Idempotent on the box's nonce. The confirm pays only for the booking its quote named (`price_changed` otherwise, and for a confirm with no quote). Two members racing for one booking: one pays, the other is told `already_paid`. A booking never charged is `not_chargeable`. Split bookings are refused (`split_booking`) — they are paid in the app.

A platform admin can additionally:
- Onboard a new tenant (creates the tenant, the first admin user, the default settings).
- Impersonate any tenant admin (logged in audit trail).
- View aggregate platform metrics (across tenants).

**On `peripheral.unhealthy`, and a correction.** This class was previously marked "specified,
not emitted in v1", on the grounds that reinstating it "needs a real peripheral driver, or a
device-side liveness signal, first". **The second half of that was false**, and it was believed
for a round. The liveness signal exists and the product already used it: the court box PUSHES
`POST /api/v1/device/heartbeat`, which writes `Device.LastSeenAt`; `HeartbeatCadence` already
computed the threshold; `DeviceView` already shipped it as `offline_after_seconds`; and the
admin Devices page already rendered "offline" from it. The only missing piece was a sweep. The
*first* half remains true and is a different thing: `BiometricDeviceState.Faulted` is still set
by no driver, because `NullBiometricDevice` is the only implementation — so this event means
"the box is not reporting", never "the reader is faulty". A fingerprint reader that fails while
its box keeps heart-beating is still invisible, and will be until a driver exists. `GET
/admin/reports/peripheral-health` remains a counts report (24-hour webhook-delivery and
biometric-match totals), not a device state.

The destination of an endpoint is guarded against SSRF (`webhooks.md`), with a carve-out
for LAN consumers. **That carve-out is deployment configuration and has no admin screen,
deliberately** — the allow-list is process-wide, so one club editing it would move the
boundary for every other club on the deployment. The refusal an admin sees says exactly
that, and names the setting so they can ask their operator for it. Reasoning:
`webhooks.md` § *The LAN carve-out*.

## 11. Notifications (email)
Every player-affecting event sends an email — this is the contractual comms channel. Webhooks mirror these for programmatic consumers but never replace them. Templates live in `src/backend/Crusaders.Mainframe.Api/Email/` — `EmailTemplates.cs` says what each message is about, as blocks, and `EmailChrome` renders those blocks as the club's HTML **and** as its plain-text alternative, so the two halves cannot drift. Every template is rendered for a fictional second club by `EmailCatalogue` and held to the house rules as a set by `EmailDesignTests`; what actually leaves the process is read off a loopback socket by `EmailTransportTests`, because an `EmailMessage` is not a message.

Required emails:
- **Email verification (registration)** — also re-sendable on request from the sign-in screen, and **the only mail an applicant gets at registration**. It carries the link, then the wait, then what ends the wait, in that order.
- ~~Membership pending review (to the applicant)~~ — **merged into the verification mail and no longer sent.** Two mails went out in the same second while `registrationCopy.ts`'s three steps told the applicant, in step 1, that they had been sent *a* link and, in step 3, that the *second* mail would come once the club approved them. The approval mail was therefore the third, and the screen's own count was wrong from the moment they opened their inbox — which, for a membership that skews older and is explicitly not technology-confident, reads as evidence that something went wrong rather than as a detail. It also split the one instruction that mattered: the pending notice carried no link at all, so an applicant who opened it first was told to wait by the one of the two whose whole job was to tell them to press something. **The screen was right about what should happen and the mail was wrong**, so the mail changed. Pinned by `An_applicant_gets_exactly_one_email_which_is_the_one_the_screen_promises`.
- **New membership application (to every tenant admin)** — the "admin notified" half of §2.3. It went unsent for a long time: both registration mails went to the applicant, so the only cue an admin ever had was a pending counter on a screen they had to think to open.
- **Admin notices (R23)** — every mail addressed to the club's administrators, the application notice above included, goes through one outbox (`admin_notifications`): the row is staged **in the same save as the event** — the registration, the outage row, the overdrawn booking, the rights change, the webhook disable — so a crash can neither lose it nor send it twice, and it is mailed by `AdminNotificationWorker` to every **active admin with a confirmed address**, minus the admin whose action it was and minus those who have opted out of that kind (`user_preferences.admin_notification_optouts`). Three kinds cannot be opted out of — administrator changes, a switched-off webhook, a suspected forged code. The kinds, their titles and their mail are `AdminEventCatalogue`; the Admin overview's feed is `GET /api/v1/admin/notifications` and the toggles read `GET /api/v1/admin/notifications/kinds`. Beyond the moved mails it adds a daily reminder while applications wait past `application_reminder_hours`, a door refusal spike (the doors tile's own rule), a fault spike (`fault_spike_count` faults newly **opened** inside `fault_spike_minutes` — old faults that keep repeating are not a spike), top-up failures the club must act on (never a decline or a cancellation; one mail per reason inside `topup_failure_cooldown_minutes`), repeating-booking clashes, and an admin's booking below zero (one mail per admin and member inside `overdraft_cooldown_minutes`). Finished notices are pruned after `admin_notification_retention_days`, and never kept past the club's `retention_years`.
- **Players (D11)** — invite, share receipts (both sides of every share that moves), pending-share reminders (club cadence and the organiser's rate-limited nudge), lapse, game cancelled, taken off / share changed. They share the admin outbox (`Audience = member`), are mirrored on the member's inbox (`GET /api/v1/me/notifications`), and non-urgent ones wait for the end of quiet hours (21:00–07:00 club time, `member_quiet_*_minute`). See `players-and-splits.md` §6.
- Membership approved + magic-link sign-in
- Membership rejected with reason
- Booking confirmation + receipt PDF attached. **Both times on the receipt are the club's own wall clock** (`tenant_settings.timezone`, per `reporting.md`): it stamped "Issued … UTC" beside a "Starts" already converted, so one document carried two times two hours apart for one booking.
- **Dates in mail and new ledger comments are the club's date words** (review round 1, lane F4): `Thu 1 Oct, 10:00` in the club's zone (`Services/ClubDate`; the receipt adds the year), and a booking's ledger comment reads `Booking Court 1 · Thu 1 Oct, 10:00`. Rows written before keep the ISO form they were written with.
- **Walk-up confirmation** — court time bought at the box is a different mail from a reservation, because it may not promise what a reservation promises. It starts immediately and the same request lights the court, so the cancellation-penalty heads-up ("cancelling within N minutes … incurs a P% penalty") is false twice over: `settlement-model.md` S4 returns **nothing** once a session has started, which is what the server's own cancellation quote answers for these bookings. **It is sent AFTER the activation, never from inside the commit**: the purchase debits first and lights the court second, so a mail sent between the two said "it has already started … not refunded once the lights have been on, and there is nothing to cancel" about a court that was still dark and a booking that was still refundable. Every clause of it was false on the refusal path.
- **Walk-up refused at the court** — its own short mail, sent when the activation refuses after the purchase has been charged. The purchase is undone in the same request (`settlement-model.md` §5), so what the member needs is that nothing was bought and the minutes are back; the cancellation templates are all wrong here, because nobody cancelled anything and the club did not act. A court priced at zero gets the sentence without a figure, since I10 writes no zero row for it to name.
- Cancellation (free)
- Cancellation (with penalty)
- **Cancellation made by the club on a member's behalf** — the same movement, but a different event, and it must say so. It names the club as the actor, carries the reason the admin recorded, and says when a penalty was waived. **The reason and the waiver are two separate facts and are not gated on each other**: the reason travels whenever the desk typed one, including when the settlement waived nothing in the end — an admin ticking the box outside the penalty window, or a member who scanned in between the quote and the confirm. Gating the reason on the waiver deleted it from the mail and from the audit row at once, and the audit row is the club's only record of why it cancelled somebody's court. On that `booking.cancelled` row the desk's words are always `reason`; the separate `waive_reason` key appears just for a cancel that actually waived a penalty (R17 E17-7; rows before R17 carry the words under `waive_reason` alone, and readers fall back to it); the desk's `booking.cancelled_by_admin` change record says what the cancel did — refund, penalty, waiver, started, who had checked in (`minutes-and-packages.md` §3.14). **`bookings.cancellation_reason` is not a second copy of it, and it is not free text**: that column is the system's own displacement marker — the resale path writes `ResoldTailRefunded` into it and `settlement-model.md` I5/S8 read it back as the discriminator that makes a displaced booking's `Owed` zero — and the admin-cancel path writes nothing to it at all. So the desk's typed words live in the audit row or nowhere, and the column is neither dead schema nor somewhere to start putting them. The cancellation itself reached the member as a mail indistinguishable from one they sent themselves — no actor, no reason, no waiver — while the audit row beside it carried all three; email is the only channel the member has, because the schedule grid just shows the slot free again. **When the club cancels a session that had started** (somebody checked in, so nothing is refunded), the mail names who checked in (R16 E16-3): "Pat Smith had checked in already, so the session counts as started and nothing was refunded", or "You had checked in already…" to the member who did — never "Somebody", which it once said even to the organiser who scanned. The checker is the seat stamped at the booking's check-in, else the first `court.activated` scanner for the booking (a pay-for payer holds no seat), else the booker. The inbox line reads "— it had started (Pat Smith had checked in / you had checked in), so nothing was refunded", and the booker's `cancelled_no_refund` wallet frame carries `checked_in_by_name` (null when it was the booker).
- **No-show with penalty** — its own template, not the cancellation one. The member cancelled nothing, a `NoShowPenalty` is not a late-cancellation penalty, and the court is not renamed. **It quotes the two ledger legs and never a netted figure**: `CancellationRefund +P` and `NoShowPenalty −penalty`, exactly as `settlement-model.md` I11 requires them to be posted. It quoted the penalty already subtracted — "Refunded 35.00 credits" against a `+50.00` row (pre-P13 wording) — which appears nowhere in the ledger, reads as a double deduction beside the penalty line, and travelled on `booking.no_show` to every programmatic consumer as well. **Subject "Missed booking — Court 1 on Tue 13 Oct at 06:00"** and a **When** row under Court (R16 E16-4): a member with two bookings on one court can tell which one was missed.
- **No-show reversed** and **Outage refund reversed** — the box's check-in reached the club after the sweep, so the booking stands as attended: the refund is taken back (and a no-show's penalty given back), naming the member whose scan did it. Like the no-show mail, each names the court **and** the day and time: subject "No-show reversed — Court 1 on Tue 13 Oct at 06:00" / "Outage refund reversed — …", and a When row (R16 E16-4; `EmailTemplates.CourtSlot`).
- Minutes adjusted at the desk; package sold at the desk
- Package purchase confirmation
- **Purchase not completed** — declined, cancelled at the gateway, or settled for an amount other than the one it was registered for. Confirmation was emailed and failure was silent, so the one terminal outcome of a member handing over card details that sent nothing was the one where they most needed telling: a member who closed the tab after PayFast could only learn what happened by finding the status page again. `TopUpStatus.tsx` promises this mail in as many words on the branch where it cannot reach the club ("we'll email you either way"), which is exactly the branch where it is the only channel left. Cancellations get it too, because "either way" is what the screen says and a member who backed out still deserves to know nothing was taken. **Once per top-up, not once per ITN retry** — PayFast retries a notification until it is acknowledged and the handler's idempotency check short-circuits only on `Completed`. `provider_register_failed` is excluded: it happens while the member is still looking at the top-up form and reading the error, and no status page exists for it to promise anything from.
- Password reset link
- Magic-link sign-in
- Admin-issued temporary password
- **Password changed** — sent on every self-service change, including the member's own. It is the one signal a member has that somebody else changed it.
- Peripheral failure auto-refund notice

## 12. Mobile-first PWA expectations
- Installable as a PWA on iOS and Android home screens.
- **The offline shell covers the app itself, not the member's data.** Opening the installed app
  with no signal gets the interface — the shell, the build assets, the icons — rather than the
  browser's offline page. Balances, bookings, the ledger and the profile all require the network.
  This line previously read "functional offline for read-only operations (view today's bookings,
  view balance from cache)", and that promise was withdrawn on 2026-09-05 because it cannot be
  kept safely on a shared device: the browser's Cache API keys entries on the request URL and
  ignores the `Authorization` header, and its storage belongs to the browser profile rather than
  to a login. A club-desk tablet therefore served the second member to open the wallet the first
  member's balance and ledger, and a cached session probe could boot the app under the first
  member's identity and admin flag. **No response carrying member data may be cached by the
  service worker.**
- **The narrowing is ratified on its merits, and the reason first recorded for it was false.**
  That reason said per-session cache keying — a cache named for the token's subject, every other
  session's cache evicted on first sight of a new one — had to be rejected because it could not
  be exercised in this repo's automation. It can: a service worker registers on the first attempt
  in the Chromium Playwright already drives here, and `src/frontend/e2e/service-worker.spec.ts`
  now registers the real worker, reproduces the two-member leak against the old version and holds
  the current rule. What the decision actually rests on, corrected 2026-09-05: (a) not caching
  member data has no failure mode, while per-session keying has one that is silent — the cache
  still answers, just with the wrong member's body; (b) it would make a privacy boundary depend on
  the page's own guess at who is signed in, at the moments the page is least sure (a token that
  expired while the tab was closed, a session ended in another tab, an admin acting on behalf of a
  member); (c) what it would buy is a stale balance, and a balance that is not the ledger is the
  one number this system may not show — nor could a member act on it, because every booking,
  cancellation and top-up needs the network. **The price is accepted and is real:** a mobile-first
  PWA whose members cannot read their balance where there is no signal. Revisit only with a design
  that survives two members sharing one device — and it is now testable, so a revisit must arrive
  with the test.
- Push notifications: out of scope for v1 — emails carry the load. Revisit post-pilot.
