# Crusaders Mainframe — Court-Light System: Round 3 Adversarial Review (post-hardening wave)

**Date:** 2026-09-05 · **Scope:** branch `claude/e2e-fix-programme` at `da6bc8c` (eight hardening-wave commits after the 2026-09-04 closing re-review) · **Method:** closure audit of every closing finding and lead and re-confirmation of the original 30, then adversarial finders across six dimensions with two independent verifiers per surviving finding · **Constraint honored:** the physical wiring is fixed; no finding below proposes changing it, and every fix direction was checked for that. · **Bookkeeping:** the brief's "23 closing findings" is the pre-merge finder count; the committed re-review carries 21 numbered findings (1 critical, 7 high, 12 medium, 1 low) plus 20 appendix leads. Both are audited below.

## 1. Verdict

"The end-to-end system is now flawless given the fixed wiring" is false, and this time the falsity is of the wave's own making. The wave did what it was asked: the critical and every high from the closing re-review are closed with pinned tests — the store-held WiFi credential, the 30-day event bound, the `!AUTH` binding, the OFFER clock gate, the `/firmware/` route and forwarded host, re-claim after revoke, per-member rate-limit partitions, the Wire timeout and watchdog on the Mega — and 18 of the 21 findings and 19 of the 20 leads are closed outright, with only F11 (plain HTTP, no response authentication) untouched and F13/L9 partially closed. The original 30 stay closed; the four partials are memory-store and comment residue, not code. But the ledger this pass produced is **0 critical / 6 high / 17 medium / 2 low**, against the committed re-review's 1 / 7 / 12 / 1 — the top of the ledger is lighter by one grade, the middle is heavier, and **19 of the 25 surviving findings were introduced by the wave itself**, most of them as direct side effects of a named closing fix: the F2 reversal grew a manual-compensation heuristic that suppresses the whole refund leg (a played court becomes free) and a displacement that ignores the box's own release; the F17 "charge what ran" bills granted minutes plus cue window on top of a retained late-cancel penalty, so a member pays more than the court ever cost; the F15 narrowing of booking codes left the Court Code page handing out exactly the code that walk-ups and pay-for-a-friend now refuse, locking out every member with a booking inside ~2 h 10 min; the F1 credential store yields to `secrets.h` on every boot, so the documented `!WIFI` rotation strands every bench-flashed box at the next power blip; the F8 hardening moved `lcd_.init()` ahead of the timeout and the watchdog, and then wrote a go-live bench check that its own timeout makes unfirable; the F7 fix stops one hop short of the Cloudflare tunnel, so the entire remote membership still shares one 10/min auth bucket. Two pre-existing high hazards were also missed by both prior passes: `iot-sign` never checks the private key against the committed public key, so a CI-green release can loop every box on UPDATING / UPDATE FAILED forever, and the gateway config that carries two of the closing fixes is a host bind-mount the release chain never delivers. The honest statement is: the system now survives the failures the last review named, and in fixing them it acquired a new set of money-direction and fleet-operations defects that are each narrower but not less real at a club. It must not go live until the six highs are closed, and the money path (findings 2, 3, 14, 16, 17) needs a single coherent settlement model rather than another layer of special cases.

### HIGH

**1. A `!WIFI` rotation on a `secrets.h`-flashed box survives only until the next power cycle — the compiled-in credential wins every boot.** *Wave (`CredentialStore.cpp` is new on the branch).*
Files: `src/iot/lib/bridgelogic/src/CredentialStore.cpp:140-152`; `src/iot/src/court_bridge/main.cpp:1598-1605,1251-1266,1268-1283`; `docs/go-live-checklist.md:104-118,184-195`; `test_bridgelogic.cpp:1758-1763`.
`chooseWifiCredential` returns `CompiledIn` whenever the macro is present, unconditionally; `setup()` calls it with `WIFI_SSID`/`WIFI_PASSWORD` on every boot; `handleWifiLine` writes the store and joins but persists no "store outranks macro" marker; `pumpWifiLink` acts only on the up-edge and there is no join-failure fallback (`WiFi.persistent(false)` removes the SDK's own copy). The host test pins the wrong precedence ("a rotated password in a fresh secrets.h wins over the store"). Go-live item 7 says "No image rebuild is needed for the second path" — false for every path-1 box until a newer credential-free release has shipped, and `ota::decide` only installs a *newer* version, so that window is open-ended.
*Club failure:* boxes bench-flashed with `secrets.h`, club rotates the WiFi password, technician types `!WIFI` at each box per item 7, sees WIFI SAVED, done. The next breaker trip reboots every ESP into the stale password; nothing joins, roster is RAM-only, every scan reads NO CONNECTION / SEE THE DESK, and no OTA can reach the boxes. Recovery is a USB reflash of every box — the intervention the store was built to avoid.
*Fix (wiring-preserving):* make the store authoritative once it holds a credential (`chooseWifiCredential` prefers `stored` when present; compiled-in is a first-boot seed only), or fall back to the store after N seconds of failing to join with the compiled-in one. Pin: `secrets.h` A, `!WIFI` B, reboot → joins B. Correct items 5/7 and `CredentialStore.h:117`.

**2. The manual-compensation heuristic skips the whole reversal on any positive adjustment naming the booking — a member who played keeps the full court refund, and the only correctly-signed repair is double-applied.** *Wave (`85524f3`). Two finders converged; merged.*
Files: `src/backend/Crusaders.Mainframe.Api/Services/BookingService.cs:775-779` (`SettledLegsAsync`); `Endpoints/WalletEndpoints.cs:94-112`; `Tests/NoShowWorkerTests.cs:348-388`; `src/frontend/src/pages/AdminMembers.tsx:401-431,453-478`.
The query matches `RelatedBookingId == booking.Id && Type == AdminAdjustment && AmountCredits > 0`, ids only, no amount read, no `UserId`; one hit returns `SettledLegs(0m, 0m, true, …)` — both money legs dropped. The sweep (`NoShowWorker.cs:100-117`) refunds the *full* charge and debits the penalty, so a member who played holds +charge −0.3·charge and the honest outstanding is always negative; a positive adjustment can never be the correct repair, and the sign-only heuristic therefore matches only wrong-direction ones. The wave's own test enshrines it: 100 seeded, 8 charged, sweep, desk returns the 2.40 penalty naming the booking, box proves attendance, asserts balance **100** — a played 8-credit court, free. The frontend `RelatedBookingField` copy actively steers the desk to this exact action.
*Club failure:* router down through a league evening; sweep refunds and penalises everyone who played; desk returns the penalties and picks the booking as the form tells them; the morning upload restores attendance, skips both legs, audits `manual_compensation`, emits `booking.no_show_reversed` with `refund_credits 0`. Club is a full court price short per member, visible only in audit JSON. Conversely a desk that posts the correct −70 is not `> 0`, the reversal runs, the member is charged twice and their next booking is refused on balance.
*Fix:* net per leg — always post the refund reversal; `penaltyBack = max(0, penalised − Σ positive adjustments naming the booking)`; record the ids on the audit row; fix the test expectation to 92; require `l.UserId == payerUserId`. See also 17.

**3. The cancelled-then-lit charge bills *granted* minutes (slot + cue window), ignores the box's own release, and stacks on a retained late-cancel penalty — the member pays more than the booking ever cost.** *Wave (`85524f3`).*
Files: `Endpoints/DeviceEventEndpoints.cs:125,344-351,383-390`; `Services/BookingService.cs:473-484,519-566,625,1029-1058`; `src/iot/lib/roster/include/roster/Roster.h:327`; `lib/court/include/court/Session.h:236-243,274`; `lib/eventq/src/Event.cpp:42`; `main.cpp:734-739`.
Offline, the roster grants `closesAt − scan` = time to `EndsAt` from a window that opens at `StartsAt − cue` (default 10 min), and the activation's `duration_ms` is that grant. `LitMinutes` = `min(ceil(duration), ceil(OriginalEndsAt − scannedAt))`, no cap at sold minutes, no lower bound at `StartsAt`, and the Cancelled branch has no cue/too-early gate (the NoShow branch directly above it has one). `ChargeLitCancelledBookingAsync` posts `SlicePrice × minutes / SliceMinutes` and, per its own doc, "does not net" against the refund — nothing nets it against a `LateCancellationPenalty` either. The box queues a `release` event with `heldMs` for every lighting; `UploadAsync` settles only `kind == "activation"` and receipts the release unread. The only tests scan after start with `waivePenalty: true` and a fixed 45-min duration.
*Club failure:* 60-min, 100-credit court cancelled inside the penalty window (club keeps 30). Member scans at 17:52 during a WiFi drop with the still-cached code: 68 lit minutes charged = 113 credits, total 143 for a 100-credit court, with an email asserting "lights ran 68 min" whether the relay ran 30 s or the full hour. Even a punctual full hour is 130.
*Fix:* cap `LitMinutes` at sold minutes and at `[StartsAt, OriginalEndsAt]`; consume the release for the same device/relay (settle activation+release as a pair or defer until the release lands); net against any retained penalty so total ≤ original price; pin early-scan, early-OFF and late-cancel cases.

**4. Every member reaching the club through the Cloudflare tunnel still shares one rate-limit bucket — the per-member fix reaches LAN clients only.** *Pre-existing root; the wave's fix stopped one hop short.*
Files: `Program.cs:127-149,172-207,341,380-381`; `nginx/nginx.conf:21`; `docs/specs/deployment.md:122-124`; `docker-compose.yml:85-88`; `AuthEndpoints.cs:17`; `appsettings.json:69`; `RateLimitTests.cs:147-200`; `memory/decisions.md:64`.

Reproduced empirically by a verifier with the identical `ForwardedHeadersOptions` block: two-hop header → bridge address under the default, → visitor with `ForwardLimit = 2`. Unauthenticated auth calls (login, register, magic-link, reset) all land in `ip:<bridge>` at 10/min; the 600/min global limiter is keyed the same way. `decisions.md:64` records #7 as closed. (Finder's "active sessions randomly logged out" sub-claim is wrong — `/refresh` runs authenticated one minute before expiry; the login lockout stands.)
*Club failure:* bookings open at 08:00; the 11th login in any minute across the whole remote membership is 429 until the minute rolls; any one outsider locks every remote member out of login with ten POSTs a minute; audit rows record one IP for all internet users. Latent today (tunnel not yet stood up), certain the day the documented deployment goes up.
*Fix:* `ForwardLimit = 2` (the bridge address is already inside the trusted `/12`), or nginx `real_ip_header CF-Connecting-IP` + forward `$remote_addr` only; add a two-entry XFF test asserting distinct partitions; state the hop count in `deployment.md`. Note the coupling with finding 15.

**5. `iot-sign` signs with whatever `OTA_PRIVATE_KEY` holds and nothing checks it against the committed public key — a mismatched secret ships a CI-green release every box rejects on every heartbeat, forever.** *Pre-existing gap, first exercisable now that #5 made the download reachable.*
Files: `.github/workflows/ci.yml:270,407-438,445-499`; `src/iot/tools/pio_sign_post.py:29-52`; `src/iot/tools/sign_firmware.py:86-110`; `src/iot/src/court_bridge/main.cpp:1349-1407,1499-1503`; `lib/otacore/src/UpdatePolicy.cpp:41-72`; `lib/otacore/src/BootGuard.cpp:70-115`; `DeviceEndpoints.cs:104-107`; `docs/go-live-checklist.md:132-146`.

The checklist prescribes exactly the two uncoupled acts that produce a mismatch (set the secret; separately commit the regenerated header), and the currently committed header's private half is on record as unlocatable, so regeneration at go-live is certain. *Club failure:* CI green, image deployed; every box paints UPDATING / DO NOT POWER OFF over the session screen, blocks in a download, paints UPDATE FAILED / SCAN TO START, repeats at every heartbeat (15–300 s) until someone reverts the image. *Fix:* `%llu` from the accumulator and a 64-bit `Event::uptimeMs` — both fields together, or post-wrap events read as 49.7 days old and hit `MaxEventAge`; or server-side modulo-2³² subtraction. Pin a corpus vector across the wrap.

## 5. What remains genuinely out of software's reach

- **Whether this board's stk500v2 bootloader survives a WDT reset** (`Watchdog.h:14-27` concedes it). Only a bench test proves it, and finding 11 shows the bench test in the checklist no longer exercises it. Until a real stall test exists, the Mega's one hard-safety property is unverified on every unit.
- **The real I2C wedge and nibble-desync rate** beside four relay coils, and whether a PCF8574 caught mid-ACK holds SDA across a Mega-only reset on this specific backpack (findings 10, 12). Software can bound the damage; only the bench measures the rate.
- **9 V rail behaviour under ESP transmit bursts** with the LCD on the separate 5 V buck (fixed wiring; go-live item 8). A Mega-only brown-out is the trigger for finding 10 and cannot be ruled in or out from the tree.
- **The physical DIP-block procedure** — every first-flash and provisioning step depends on a technician setting eight switches correctly in the right order; findings 9 and 19 show the docs mis-describe what they will then see, but no software can verify the switch positions.
- **The seven Actions secrets and the OTA key pair** are operator acts; finding 5 gives CI a way to detect a mismatch but not to prevent the operator minting two pairs on two machines.
- **The go-live S4 physical checks** stand, with the stale JWT-QR bullet noted in §2c.
